## Ukraine / Russia

### Putin remains uncompromising on Ukraine, but is public discourse on war changing in Russia?

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-03
**Category:** Ukraine/Russia

**Summary:**
BBC Russia Editor Steve Rosenberg examines Putin's unchanging public position — demanding Ukraine cede the entire Donbas — even as the war enters its fifth year with growing strains on Russia's economy and society. Ukrainian drones now reach deep inside Russia, targeting oil refineries and energy infrastructure. A large-scale drone attack on the Moscow region last month raised fears about capital air defences. Victory Day celebrations were scaled back amid attack concerns. Budget deficits are growing, the economy stagnating, and there are rising shortages of air defence personnel. Meanwhile, the "spirit of Anchorage" — the hoped-for US pressure on Ukraine that Moscow expected from Trump — has faded, with Putin's aide publicly distancing the Kremlin from the phrase.

**Key points:**
- Putin displays no regret and no intention of ceasing hostilities; continues to demand full Donbas
- Russia's war has become a costly attritional conflict: huge battlefield losses, economy under strain, technological decline
- Ukrainian drones now reach deep inside Russian territory — oil refineries and Moscow region targeted
- St Petersburg International Economic Forum underway (Jun 3–6), with delegations from 130+ countries despite war
- "Spirit of Anchorage" era over — Moscow no longer confident Trump will deliver peace on Russia's terms
- Growing domestic压力: budget deficit, shortages, 41,000+ Kyiv residents sheltering in metro overnight during recent strikes

### Seven killed after drone hits bus in Russia-controlled part of Ukraine

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-03
**Category:** Ukraine/Russia

**Summary:**
A Ukrainian drone struck a civilian passenger bus travelling between Moscow and Simferopol in Russian-occupied Donetsk, killing seven people and injuring 11. Russia said it shot down 350 Ukrainian drones overnight across multiple regions. Ukraine is yet to formally claim responsibility. **Key points:**
- 7 killed, 11 injured in Donetsk drone strike on civilian bus; unclaimed by Ukraine
- St Petersburg oil terminal also hit by Ukrainian drones on same day (Jun 3)
- Russia claims 350 Ukrainian drones shot down overnight during mass attack
- 50+ drones downed over Leningrad region (includes St Petersburg)
- Zelensky confirmed St Petersburg strike, posting video of smoke rising over the city
- Attack came a day after a massive Russian assault killed at least 22 people across Ukraine

### Sailors stressed and exhausted after months trapped by Strait of Hormuz blockade

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-02
**Category:** Middle East / Strait of Hormuz

**Summary:**
About 20,000 sailors across 1,600 ships have been trapped in or near the Strait of Hormuz since the USIsraeli war with Iran began in late February. The US naval blockade prevents ships from leaving the Gulf; Iran shut the strait to commercial traffic. Crews face psychological distress, water shortages, and extortionate supply costs (water prices up 600%). Two ships attempted to cross after the April 8 ceasefire announcement; both turned back after Iran reversed its opening decision in response to the US maintaining its port blockade. seafarers endure heat over 30°C, with summer bringing temperatures up to 45°C. The IMO estimates 1,600 vessels stranded on the wrong side of the Strait.

**Key points:**
- ~20,000 sailors on ~1,600 ships trapped in Gulf by US blockade and Iranian strait closure since late February
- Ships cannot leave: Iran controls only exit from the Gulf; US controls access to Iranian ports
- Water costs increased 600% ($1,500-2,000 pre-war → $11,000 for same quantity now)
- Two ships attempted crossing after April 8 ceasefire; Iran reversed opening after US maintained port blockade
- Crews report constant anxiety, sleep disruption, and isolation; mental health crisis emerging
- Ships moving to Omani ports for supplies but deliveries increasingly unpredictable and expensive
- 1,600 IMO-tracked vessels estimated stranded, with real number likely higher
