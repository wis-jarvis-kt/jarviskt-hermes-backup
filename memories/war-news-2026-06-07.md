## Ukraine / Russia

### Ukrainian drones hit St Petersburg during SPIEF economic forum

**Source:** BBC —2026-06-07
**Category:** Ukraine / Russia

**Summary:**
Ukrainian drones attacked the St Petersburg area on the opening day of Russia's flagship International Economic Forum (SPIEF), producing a large plume of thick black smoke visible across the city skyline. Separately, President Zelensky published an open letter to Putin proposing direct peace talks at a neutral venue; Putin rejected the offer, calling the letter "rude" and deflecting to frontline soldiers.

**Key points:**
- Ukrainian one-way attack drones struck infrastructure near St Petersburg on the opening day of SPIEF (June 3–6), forcing delegates to witness the smoke plume on arrival.
- Local officials acknowledged infrastructure damage but did not specify targets.
- Zelensky proposed Putin meet in a third country; Putin responded that there is "no point" meeting Zelensky and told frontline troops to "keep at it."
- Putin used SPIEF to project confidence, claiming Russia's economy is "developing" and "stable" despite war and sanctions.
- The drone attacks underscore Ukraine's growing long-range strike capability deep inside Russian territory.

**Why it matters:** The attacks on SPIEF — Russia's premier international economic showcase — are a deliberate embarrassment and demonstrate Ukraine's ability to penetrate even heavily defended targets near Russia's second city. Putin's rejection of direct talks signals Moscow remains unwilling to negotiate on terms Kyiv or the West would accept, keeping the diplomatic track deadlocked as battlefield activity continues.

### Armenia votes amid mounting Russian economic pressure

**Source:** BBC —2026-06-07
**Category:** Ukraine / Russia

**Summary:**
Armenians voted on June 7 in a snap election that could determine whether the country continues its pro-West trajectory under PM Nikol Pashinyan or reverts toward Russia's orbit. Moscow has imposed targeted trade bans on Armenian flowers, mineral water, cognac, and fresh produce in the two weeks preceding the vote.

**Key points:**
- Pashinyan is seeking a third term on a platform of EU accession and Western integration; his domestic support has fallen from 54% (2021) to ~30% amid anger over his concessions to Azerbaijan that led to the loss of Nagorno-Karabakh.
- His main challenger is billionaire Samvel Karapetyan, who made his fortune in Russia and is under house arrest on charges of plotting to overthrow the government.
- Russia banned exports of Armenian flowers, mineral water, cognac, fresh vegetables, and fruit two weeks before the election — a direct economic pressure tactic.
- Russia accounted for 36% of Armenia's foreign trade in 2025.
- The US-brokered peace deal with Azerbaijan has won Trump's endorsement for Pashinyan.

**Why it matters:** The election is a proxy battle between Western-backed democratic reform and Russian economic coercion in the South Caucasus. A Pashinyan victory would consolidate Armenia's Western tilt; a win for pro-Russian opposition forces would represent a significant strategic reversal for the US and EU in a region long considered within Russia's sphere of influence.

## Middle East

### US and Iran exchange strikes in the Gulf — ceasefire under renewed strain

**Source:** BBC — 2026-06-07
**Category:** Middle East

**Summary:**
The shaky US-Iran ceasefire has been tested again by reciprocal strikes. US forces shot down four Iranian one-way attack drones heading toward the Strait of Hormuz and struck Iranian radar sites on Sirik and Qeshm Island. Iran responded by firing seven ballistic missiles at two US air bases in Kuwait and US Navy facilities in Bahrain; six were intercepted and one reached its target without causing major damage. Ceasefire negotiations have stalled, with the US reportedly requesting changes to the terms.

**Key points:**
- The exchange follows the same pattern as several prior incidents since the April ceasefire: drone launches, US counter-strikes, Iranian missile retaliation.
- Centcom said the Iranian drones "posed an immediate threat to regional maritime traffic" in the Strait of Hormuz, through which ~20% of the world's oil and Liquefied Natural Gas passes.
- Iran condemned the US strikes as a "flagrant violation" of the ceasefire and "an attack on national sovereignty."
- Kuwait and Bahrain condemned the Iranian attacks; the UAE and Qatar also denounced them.
- Despite the hostilities, the US granted visas to Iran's World Cup football team for their match in Los Angeles on June 15 — the first time a host nation has received a team from a country it is at war with.
- Ceasefire talks have stalled; Iran says the US keeps "changing its views and putting forward new or contradictory demands."

**Why it matters:** The reciprocal strike cycle demonstrates that the April ceasefire is at best a pause in hostilities, not a resolution. With both sides unable or unwilling to agree on endgame terms, the pattern of drone-and-missile exchanges threatens to escalate. The Strait of Hormuz remains a critical chokepoint — any sustained disruption would send oil prices soaring globally.

### Israeli strike kills Lebanese army general and two soldiers in south Lebanon

**Source:** BBC — 2026-06-07
**Category:** Middle East

**Summary:**
The Israeli military struck a vehicle carrying Lebanese Army soldiers near the village of Kfar Tebnit in southern Lebanon, killing a brigadier-general, a captain, and a private. The IDF said the vehicle was "moving suspiciously towards forces" in an active combat zone and that it was investigating the incident. Lebanon's army called the strike "aggressive and barbaric." The attack comes as Israel continues to fight Hezbollah in southern Lebanon, separate from but linked to the broader US-Israel-Iran war.

**Key points:**
- Victims identified as Brig. Gen. Samer Sabra, Cpt. Elie Khoury, and Pte. Hassan Ghazal.
- Israel is not formally at war with the Lebanese government, which is engaged in US-mediated ceasefire talks with Israel — but Israel is fighting Hezbollah, which launched rockets into Israel on March 2 in retaliation for a strike that killed Iran's supreme leader.
- Hezbollah's leader Naim Qassem has rejected the US-backed ceasefire framework, calling the talks "futile."
- The IDF says it struck ~150 Hezbollah infrastructure sites in southern Lebanon over the weekend, including weapon storage and command centres.
- At least 3,550 people have been killed in Lebanon since the war began; Israeli casualties stand at 26 soldiers and 4 civilians.

**Why it matters:** The killing of Lebanese soldiers by Israeli forces complicates the diplomatic effort to separate Hezbollah from Lebanon's state structure, which the US sees as key to any durable ceasefire. The IDF's insistence it was targeting a "suspicious" vehicle — and its acknowledgment that the family were "uninvolved civilians" — highlights the ongoing friction between Israeli operations and Lebanese government sovereignty.

### Funeral held for seven-month-old Palestinian baby shot dead by Israeli troops in Hebron

**Source:** BBC — 2026-06-07
**Category:** Middle East

**Summary:**
A funeral was held in Hebron for seven-month-old Sam Fahd Abu Haikal, shot dead by an Israeli soldier while in a car with his family on Friday evening in the Tel Rumeida area of occupied West Bank. The family says they obeyed soldiers' orders to stop; the IDF says forces "perceived a vehicle accelerating towards them" and fired a single shot. The IDF has acknowledged the family were "uninvolved civilians" and expressed "deep sorrow," but the father has dismissed the regret as insufficient given multiple bullets were fired without warning.

**Key points:**
- The car was struck by bullets that penetrated the front windshield; the bullet went through the father's arm and then struck baby Sam in the head. The mother was also hit and remains in hospital.
- Tel Rumeida is a flashpoint area of Hebron where Israeli settlers live among Palestinian residents under heavy military protection.
- Over 1,000 Palestinians have been killed in the West Bank since October 7, 2023, according to the Palestinian health ministry.
- The IDF says the incident is under review; the soldier who fired has not been publicly identified.

**Why it matters:** The killing of a infant in an occupied territory under military rule — even if ultimately deemed accidental — underscores the extreme volatility and civilian toll in the West Bank. The IDF's acknowledgment that the family were uninvolved civilians but its failure to explain why multiple soldiers fired without a warning shot is likely to intensify calls for accountability and international scrutiny.

### Israel and Lebanon agree conditional ceasefire framework — Hezbollah rejects it

**Source:** BBC — 2026-06-04
**Category:** Middle East

**Summary:**
Israel and Lebanon agreed to implement a conditional ceasefire contingent on Hezbollah ceasing all attacks, with the US brokering a framework that would establish "pilot security zones" inside southern Lebanon from which Hezbollah operatives would be banned. The Lebanese Armed Forces would take "exclusive control" of those zones. Hezbollah's leader rejected the deal as "futile," and Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu subsequently ordered an intensification of strikes.

**Key points:**
- The agreement (announced June 4) did not include maps or operational details on where the pilot zones would be located.
- Israel would refrain from bombing Beirut if Hezbollah did not attack Israel — the initial partial ceasefire condition.
- Far-right Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir called the agreement a "serious mistake."
- The two countries' representatives are scheduled to meet again on June 22 for further talks.
- The initial April16 ceasefire failed to stop the fighting; escalation resumed within days.

**Why it matters:** The US-backed ceasefire framework is the second attempt to halt hostilities in Lebanon; its rejection by Hezbollah leaves Israel with no diplomatic off-ramp and no reason to reduce its military campaign. The deepening of the conflict in Lebanon is directly linked to the US-Israel-Iran war, as Iran uses Hezbollah as its primary regional military instrument.

## Taiwan / South China Sea

### Japan-Philippines sign major defense agreements; strengthen first island chain

**Source:** Institute for the Study of War — 2026-06-05
**Category:** Taiwan / South China Sea

**Summary:**
Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. met Japanese PM Sanae Takaichi in Tokyo on May 28 and signed a series of defense agreements upgrading bilateral relations to a "Comprehensive Strategic Partnership." Key outcomes include accelerated talks on military intelligence sharing, Philippine plans to acquire six Japanese "Mogami"-class destroyers specialized for anti-submarine warfare, and Japan's announcement it will discuss transferring Type-88 anti-ship missiles to the Philippines. Japan and the Philippines also announced they would negotiate EEZ delimitation in the Western Pacific — an area overlapping with PRC and Taiwan's claimed EEZs east of Taiwan.

**Key points:**
- The military intelligence sharing agreement would give both nations a more comprehensive operating picture of the first island chain, increasing awareness of PLA deployments.
- Japan's transfer of advanced surface combatants to the Philippines would substantially upgrade the Philippine Navy's ability to confront PRC maritime coercion in the South China Sea.
- EEZ delimitation talks between Japan and the Philippines could create friction with PRC claims; it is unclear whether PRC or Taiwan would be included in any boundary negotiations.
- Both Japan and the Philippines adhere to a one-China policy, limiting official engagement with Taiwan.

**Why it matters:** The strengthening Japan-Philippines defense relationship is the most significant bilateral security development in the first island chain this year. It directly addresses PRC coercive activity in the South China Sea by providing the Philippines with higher-end naval capabilities and better intelligence integration with Japan. The move constrains PLA freedom of maneuver and signals that US allies are deepening operational coordination without direct US involvement.

### Taiwan developing GPS-jamming resistant drones for PLA invasion scenario

**Source:** Institute for the Study of War — 2026-06-05
**Category:** Taiwan / South China Sea

**Summary:**
Taiwan's Aerospace Industry Development Corporation (AIDC) is leading an industry alliance of over 200 Taiwanese drone manufacturers to develop the AIxVNAV visual navigation system, which allows drones to operate without GPS signals — critical given that the PLA possesses advanced electronic warfare (EW) capabilities including land and sea-based GPS jammers. The system uses AI-based visual navigation powered by satellite 3D mapping data and is designed to function in environments where GPS is disrupted or jammed.

**Key points:**
- Russia and Ukraine have demonstrated that GPS jamming and spoofing can disrupt enemy drones and precision-guided munitions; this lesson is directly applicable to a Taiwan scenario.
- The PLA views electromagnetic spectrum dominance as a key precondition for an amphibious invasion of Taiwan.
- Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs established the Taiwan Excellence Drone International Business Opportunities Alliance (TEDIBOA) in 2024 to reduce reliance on PRC-origin drone components.
- AIDC developed AIxVNAV using licensed tools from US geospatial intelligence firm Vantor.

**Why it matters:** A successful PLA invasion of Taiwan would likely begin with intensive electronic warfare to blind and disrupt Taiwan's military communications and drone systems. Taiwan's investment in GPS-jamming-resistant drone technology is a direct hedge against this scenario and reflects lessons from the Ukraine war. At scale, EW-resistant drones could allow Taiwan to locate, surveil, and strike PLA forces even under heavy electronic attack.
