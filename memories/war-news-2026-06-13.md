## Middle East

### Israel carries out air strikes on Lebanon as Iran claims US deal is close

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-13
**Category:** Middle East

**Summary:**
Israel carried out air strikes in southern Lebanon on June 13, Lebanese state media reported, ordering evacuations from about 20 locations. At least one person was killed in a strike on the town of Marrakeh in the Tyre district. The strikes came hours after Iran's Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi said a deal to end fighting between the US and Iran — and between Israel and Hezbollah — was close.

**Key points:**
- The alleged deal would reopen the Strait of Hormuz and lift the US blockade on Iranian shipping in exchange for Iran halting enrichment and destroying enriched uranium on site
- US officials confirmed some details of the agreement, saying economic benefits would depend on Tehran meeting obligations
- A 60-day negotiation period on Iran's nuclear programme would follow
- Despite a April ceasefire, the US and Iran have exchanged intermittent fire this week, including two rounds of tit-for-tat strikes
- Israel is not involved in the US-Iran talks

**Why it matters:** A US-Iran deal could de-escalate the broader regional conflict, but Israel appears to be continuing its campaign against Hezbollah independently, risking a rupture in the emerging agreement.

### US and Iran exchange strikes for second day; oil prices spike

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-11
**Category:** Middle East

**Summary:**
The US and Iran exchanged strikes across the Middle East for a second consecutive day on June 11, further straining the April ceasefire. US Central Command (Centcom) completed a wave of self-defense strikes targeting military, surveillance, and radar sites in southern Iran. Iran responded by targeting US assets in Bahrain, Kuwait, and Jordan, with Bahrain reporting damage to homes and vehicles in Manama from intercepted drone shrapnel.

**Key points:**
- Iran's IRGC said it fired 12 ballistic missiles at the Muwaffaq Salti Airbase in Jordan, destroying a "large number" of US fighter jets and facilities
- Jordan said 20 missiles were intercepted with no casualties
- Kuwait intercepted "hostile aerial targets" and briefly closed its airspace
- Iran briefly declared the Strait of Hormuz "completely closed to all type of vessel" and reportedly targeted two oil tankers; Centcom said commercial shipping continued
- Brent crude oil rose to around $95/barrel following the Hormuz disruption

**Why it matters:** The Strait of Hormuz is the world's most critical oil shipping chokepoint. The temporary closure and tanker attack signals the conflict's potential to disrupt global energy markets even without a full closure.

### US strikes Iran after helicopter downing over Strait of Hormuz

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-10
**Category:** Middle East

**Summary:**
The US carried out strikes on Iranian military and surveillance sites near the Strait of Hormuz in response to Iran downing an American Apache helicopter. The two crew members survived and were rescued by a US sea drone. US officials said Iran used a drone in the attack but it was unclear if it was deliberate. Iran's IRGC subsequently struck 21 US targets across Bahrain and Jordan.

**Key points:**
- The Apache was patrolling the Strait of Hormuz, which Iran effectively closed following the February US-Israel strikes on Iran
- Iran's Foreign Minister warned: "Leave our region if you want to be safe"
- US House Speaker Mike Johnson said he was in the room when Trump decided to resume attacks on Iran
- Jordan shot down five missiles fired from Iran; Bahrain's air raid sirens were activated

**Why it matters:** The helicopter incident was the immediate trigger for the current escalation cycle, and the tit-for-tat exchanges have persisted despite ceasefire talks.

## Ukraine / Russia

### Russia may launch Oreshnik IRBM at Ukraine in next 24–48 hours; Putin acknowledges setbacks

**Source:** Institute for the Study of War — 2026-06-12
**Category:** Ukraine/Russia

**Summary:**
The Ukrainian Air Force warned on June 12 of a "high probability" that Russian forces would launch an Oreshnik intermediate-range ballistic missile (IRBM) at Ukraine from the Kapustin Yar site within 24–48 hours. On Russia Day (June 12), President Putin met with Russian soldiers, acknowledged that forces were not advancing "as quickly as we would like," and claimed advances continue "every day gradually." ISW notes Russia's rate of advance has been declining since November 2025, with net territorial losses in April–May 2026.

**Key points:**
- The Kremlin has launched Oreshniks at least three times in the missile's operational history; at least two were in 2026
- Ukraine's long-range strike campaign against Russian logistics and oil infrastructure has disrupted Russian supply routes, particularly in southern Ukraine and into occupied Crimea
- Ukraine's counterattacks have made tactical gains in Zaporizhia and Dnipropetrovsk Oblasts since March 2026
- A Russian State Duma Deputy from the Communist Party publicly criticized the Kremlin's war strategy, contradicting official narrative
- Putin lauded Russian technological innovations (FPV drones, AI integration, tactical EW systems) but did not acknowledge the full extent of battlefield challenges

**Why it matters:** The possible Oreshnik strike would be a significant escalation timed to Russia Day, posturing strength following battlefield setbacks and Ukrainian strikes on Russian cities including St. Petersburg.

### Xi visits North Korea; signals support for Pyongyang's nuclear status

**Source:** Institute for the Study of War — 2026-06-12
**Category:** Taiwan/South China Sea

**Summary:**
Chinese President Xi Jinping visited North Korea from June 8–9, his first overseas trip in 2026. Xi and Kim Jong Un agreed to strengthen strategic coordination and "firmly safeguard each country's sovereignty, security, and development interests." Notably absent was any mention of "denuclearization" — a significant shift from past PRC policy. Kim publicly toured a new "nuclear material production plant" ahead of Xi's arrival.

**Key points:**
- Xi appears to have legitimized North Korea's nuclear program by acknowledging its "sovereignty and security" interests without referencing denuclearization
- Russia has openly supported North Korea's nuclear arsenal, pressuring the PRC to deepen engagement with Pyongyang
- The PRC omitted denuclearization language from the Xi-Kim summit and from the May 14 Trump-Xi summit readout, despite the White House stating denuclearization was discussed
- Xi meets Taiwan KMT figure Cheng during US visit; KMT does not oppose US arms sales to Taiwan

**Why it matters:** Xi's North Korea visit and omission of denuclearization language signals Beijing is accepting North Korea's nuclear status, reducing prospects for inter-Korean dialogue and increasing coordination with Russia on the Korean Peninsula.
