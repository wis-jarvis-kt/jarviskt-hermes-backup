## Middle East

### US and Iran exchange strikes across Middle East for second day in a row

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-11
**Category:** Middle East / US-Iran Conflict

**Summary:**
The US and Iran exchanged strikes across the Middle East for a second consecutive day, further straining a shaky ceasefire agreed between the two countries in April 2026. US Central Command (Centcom) completed a wave of "self-defense strikes" targeting military, surveillance and radar sites in southern Iran. Tehran responded by targeting US military assets in Bahrain, Kuwait, and Jordan with ballistic missiles. Iran's foreign ministry said the overnight attacks violated the two-month-old ceasefire, rendering it "practically meaningless."

**Key points:**
- Centcom struck air defense systems, ground control stations, and radar sites near the Strait of Hormuz
- Iran's IRGC fired 12 ballistic missiles at the Muwaffaq Salti Airbase in Jordan, claiming to have destroyed US fighter jets and facilities
- Jordanian military intercepted 20 missiles; Kuwait intercepted "hostile aerial targets" and temporarily closed its airspace
- Bahrain reported falling shrapnel from intercepted Iranian drones damaging homes in Manama and Hamad Town; an 11-year-old girl received a minor injury
- Iran's IRGC claimed it struck two oil tankers passing through the Strait of Hormuz after state media reported the channel "completely closed to all type of vessel"
- Brent crude oil rose to around $95/barrel (~2% increase)
- Iran's foreign minister warned: "Leave our region if you want to be safe"

**Why it matters:** The ceasefire negotiated in April is now under severe strain after the US resumed strikes following the downing of an American Apache helicopter. Iran's retaliatory strikes demonstrate it is willing and able to respond to US attacks on its territory, raising the risk of further escalation. The Strait of Hormuz — through which roughly 20% of global oil flows — remains a flashpoint.

### US strikes Iran in response to downing of military helicopter

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-10
**Category:** Middle East / US-Iran Conflict

**Summary:**
The US carried out a series of strikes on Iranian military and surveillance sites in response to the downing of an American Apache helicopter over the Gulf. The two US crew members survived and were rescued by an American sea drone. Iran's IRGC subsequently launched strikes on 21 targets at US bases across the region. President Trump had accused Iran of shooting down the helicopter and vowed a response, saying US forces "must, of necessity" respond.

**Key points:**
- US strikes targeted air defense systems, ground control stations, and radar sites near the Strait of Hormuz
- The Apache was downed using an Iranian drone, though it remains unclear whether the attack was deliberate (per unnamed US official to CBS News)
- Iran's IRGC struck US bases in Bahrain and Jordan; Kuwait also reported intercepting an attack
- Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi warned: "We will leave no attack or threat unanswered"
- Iran reportedly targeted the cities of Jask, Sirik, and Qeshm island in the strikes
- Jordan's military said it shot down five missiles fired from Iran
- Negotiations between the US and Iran are ongoing but now deeply complicated

**Why it matters:** The helicopter incident and subsequent exchange of fire mark a dangerous escalation. Iran has demonstrated it can strike US assets across multiple countries simultaneously. The fundamental question — whether the April ceasefire holds or collapses entirely — remains unresolved.

### Israel and Iran flare-up could strengthen Tehran's negotiating hand

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-09
**Category:** Middle East / Israel-Iran

**Summary:**
Israel bombed sites in Iran for the first time since the April ceasefire, after Iran fired missiles at Israel in what Tehran said was retaliation for Israeli strikes on Lebanon's capital, Beirut. Analysis suggests Trump gave Netanyahu a "blinking yellow light" rather than a firm instruction to stand down. The episode highlighted fractures between the US and Israel, with Trump reportedly calling Netanyahu "crazy" in a private conversation.

**Key points:**
- Israel struck Iran hours after Trump told reporters he would call Netanyahu to urge restraint
- Trump denied Netanyahu defied him, saying: "If I tell him to do something, he does it"
- Iran aimed to prise open differences between the US and Israel by striking Israeli territory
- Trump stated a nuclear deal with Iran was "in the final throes" and could come in "two or three days"
- Iranian negotiator Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf had warned: "We prefer the language of diplomacy, but we speak other languages far more fluently"
- The analysis notes Trump's appetite for risk appears currently low, which Iran is seeking to exploit at the negotiating table

**Why it matters:** Israel's independent strikes on Iran undermine Trump's diplomatic strategy and complicate efforts to reach a nuclear deal. Tehran now has additional leverage — both military (demonstrated strike capability) and negotiating (the ability to prompt Israeli escalation that threatens Trump's broader regional strategy).

### UK and allies sanction 'networks' enabling settler violence in West Bank

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-09
**Category:** Middle East / Israel-Palestine

**Summary:**
The UK, Australia, Canada, France, and Norway imposed coordinated sanctions on networks involved in financing and enabling attacks by Jewish settlers against Palestinian civilians in the Israeli-occupied West Bank. France also barred far-right Israeli Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich from entering the country. The five countries cited a 130% increase in settler attacks resulting in casualties or property damage in 2025 compared to the previous year.

**Key points:**
- The UN documented 1,835 settler attacks against Palestinians in 2025 — a 130% increase year-over-year
- At least seven Palestinians were killed and 832 injured in those attacks
- The UK sanctioned six entities and one individual, including an association providing financial support to settler farms and a construction company used to destroy Palestinian land and property
- Israel's government called the sanctions "disgraceful political acts camouflaged as measures against violence"
- The UK issued new official guidance advising businesses against economic activity in illegal settlements
- Settlement expansion has accelerated sharply since Netanyahu's coalition took power in 2022, with over 100 new settlements approved

**Why it matters:** The coordinated sanctions signal growing international impatience with Israeli settlement expansion and settler violence, particularly as the Gaza war continues. France's entry ban on Smotrich is a notable diplomatic escalation within the EU.

## South China Sea / Taiwan Strait

### China & Taiwan Update, June 5, 2026 (ISW)

**Source:** Institute for the Study of War — 2026-06-05
**Category:** South China Sea / Taiwan Strait

**Summary:**
Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. met with Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi on May 28, announcing defense agreements to strengthen ties, including accelerated talks on military intelligence sharing and the transfer of Japanese naval vessels to the Philippines. The Philippines seeks to acquire six "Maya"-class destroyers specialized for anti-submarine warfare. Japan also announced it would begin discussions over transferring Type-88 anti-ship missiles to the Philippines. The two nations agreed to upgrade relations to a "Comprehensive Strategic Partnership" and work toward EEZ delimitation negotiations in the Western Pacific.

**Key points:**
- Japan-Philippines EEZ delimitation talks will proceed east of Taiwan, overlapping with PRC and Taiwan claimed EEZs — neither PRC nor Taiwan were invited
- Both Japan and the Philippines adhere to a one-China policy, limiting official engagement with Taiwan
- Enhanced cooperation will strengthen both nations' ability to combat PRC coercion in the South China Sea
- Military intelligence sharing would give both nations better awareness of PLA deployments in the first island chain
- Taiwan's AIDC is developing AIxVNAV — an EW-resistant drone navigation system using visual navigation and AI to operate in GPS-jammed environments
- The PLA views GPS jamming as a key precondition for an amphibious invasion of Taiwan; Taiwan's defense may depend on fielding EW-resistant drones at scale

**Why it matters:** Japan's deepening defense partnership with the Philippines represents a significant expansion of regional security architecture east of Taiwan. Meanwhile, Taiwan's development of GPS-jamming-resistant drone navigation systems reflects serious investment in asymmetric defense capabilities against PLA electronic warfare.
