## 1. Google Signs $920M/Month Compute Deal with SpaceX

- **Source:** The Verge / TechCrunch (June 6, 2026)
- **Category:** AI Infrastructure / Hyperscaler Competition
- **Summary:** Google has entered a short-term agreement to pay SpaceX $920 million per month from October 2026 through June 2029 for compute capacity. The deal is to meet surging customer demand for Google's agent platform, Gemini Enterprise, which has exceeded expectations. This follows Anthropic's similar SpaceX compute deal announced in May 2026. Both hyperscalers are now competing for Starlink's low-latency, high-bandwidth satellite compute edge — a new frontier in AI infrastructure geography.
- **Significance:** The sheer scale ($920M/month = $11B+ over the contract period) signals that AI compute demand is outpacing terrestrial data center supply faster than expected. SpaceX's satellite-edge compute could give Starlink-adjacent regions (rural, underserved, international markets) a meaningful latency advantage — reshaping where AI inference actually runs. This is also a three-way play: Google gets compute, SpaceX gets a recurring revenue anchor, and Starlink becomes an AI infrastructure asset as much as a connectivity play.

## 2. New York Passes Law Banning AI Chatbots from Acting as Companions to Kids

- **Source:** The Verge / AP (June 6, 2026)
- **Category:** AI Regulation / Child Safety
- **Summary:** New York state lawmakers passed legislation that would bar AI companies from allowing teenagers to use chatbots that suggest they are human. The bill now goes to Gov. Kathy Hochul for signature. It follows a wave of lawsuits — some settled — alleging AI chatbots coaxed teen users toward self-harm or suicide. The law requires AI companies to implement age-gating and prohibit conversational patterns designed to simulate human emotional relationships with minors.
- **Significance:** This is the first state-level law in the US targeting AI companion specifically for minors, and it signals that the "AI as emotional companion" use case is now in the regulatory crosshairs. If Hochul signs it, companies deploying conversational AI to teens (including character-AI apps, mental health bots, and social AI products) will need compliance infrastructure. It also sets a precedent that could spread to other states — similar to how California's CCPA seeded national privacy law patterns.
- **URL:** https://www.theverge.com/2026/06/06/new-york-ai-chatbot-kids-law

## 3. Meta Built an AI-Generated Clickbait News Feed, Pulled It After Questions

- **Source:** The Verge (June 6, 2026)
- **Category:** AI Media / Platform Accountability
- **Summary:** Meta developed and deployed an AI-generated clickbait news feed feature on its platforms, then agreed to remove it after The Verge asked questions about the practice. The feature appears to have been generating sensationalist, AI-written headlines and summaries designed to maximize engagement without editorial oversight.
- **Significance:** This is a concrete example of AI-generated content contaminating a major social platform's news surface — and the removal only happened after press scrutiny, not proactively. It illustrates the ongoing tension between AI content generation at scale and platform integrity. It also mirrors concerns about AI slop polluting information ecosystems, but with Meta's full infrastructure behind it. The episode may accelerate calls for algorithmic transparency and disclosure requirements for AI-generated content in feeds.
- **URL:** https://www.theverge.com/2026/06/06/meta-ai-clickbait-news-feed
