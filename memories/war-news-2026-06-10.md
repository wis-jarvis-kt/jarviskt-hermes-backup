## Ukraine / Russia

### Zelensky under pressure to end row with Poland over WW2 name of army unit

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-10
**Category:** Ukraine/Russia

**Summary:**
Ukraine is facing a major diplomatic fallout with key ally Poland after Kyiv named a military unit after the Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA), a WWII-era group responsible for massacres of ethnic Poles in Volhynia. Polish President Karol Nawrocki is considering stripping Zelensky of Poland's highest state honour, the Order of the White Eagle.

**Key points:**
- Zelensky issued a decree naming a unit of Ukraine's Special Operations Forces after the UPA, which fought against the Soviet Red Army and Nazi Germany but was responsible for killing an estimated 100,000 ethnic Poles in 1943–45.
- Nawrocki condemned the naming as "glorification of bandits and killers" and is consulting with the council of the Order on whether to revoke the honour.
- Ukraine's chief of staff Kyrylo Budanov travelled to Warsaw to ease tensions, but Tusk later admitted "diplomacy has yielded no results."
- Polish opposition politicians have demanded freezing funding for Starlink services to Ukraine and blocking Ukraine's EU accession bid.
- Zelensky flew to the UK via Moldova this week rather than through Poland's Rzeszów airport, which has been his usual transit hub.

**Why it matters:** The dispute strikes at the fragile unity of Ukraine's Western coalition. Poland has sheltered nearly a million Ukrainian refugees and been one of Kyiv's most reliable supporters since the 2022 invasion. A sustained deterioration in Polish-Ukrainian relations could weaken diplomatic and military support at a critical juncture in the war.

### Russia's fuel crisis intensifies as Ukraine steps up strikes on occupied territories

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-09
**Category:** Ukraine/Russia

**Summary:**
Ukraine's sustained drone campaign against Russian-occupied territories is disrupting Moscow's supply lines and deepening a fuel crisis in Crimea. The peninsula — used by Russia to launch drones and missiles at mainland Ukraine — is experiencing severe shortages after Ukrainian strikes on the key road linking Rostov to Crimea via Mariupol.

**Key points:**
- Ukraine carried out approximately 300 drone strikes on trucks, including 30 tankers, since the start of May; military cargo traffic on the road decreased by 71% between late May and early June.
- At most Crimean petrol stations, residents are limited to purchasing 20 litres of fuel using prepaid vouchers; queues of up to 10 hours have been reported.
- Russia's energy ministry acknowledged fuel supply problems in "the southern regions" on 8 June and set up a permanent HQ to address it.
- Previous attacks have already restricted traffic on the Kerch Bridge connecting Crimea to mainland Russia; the sea route is considered too dangerous after Ukraine struck several ferries.

**Why it matters:** Crimea is strategically vital for Russia's southern strike capabilities. The fuel disruption degrades Moscow's ability to supply its occupation forces and could affect the summer holiday season for Russian tourists — a domestic political consideration for the Kremlin. The campaign demonstrates Ukraine's growing ability to disrupt Russian logistics deep behind the front lines.

## Middle East

### US strikes Iran after American helicopter downed over Strait of Hormuz

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-10
**Category:** Middle East

**Summary:**
The United States carried out strikes on Iranian military and surveillance sites near the Strait of Hormuz in response to the downing of an American Apache helicopter. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) responded by launching strikes on 21 targets at US bases in Bahrain and Jordan, while Kuwait reported intercepting an attack. The exchange marks a significant escalation in the ongoing US-Iran conflict.

**Key points:**
- US Central Command (Centcom) said American strikes targeted air defence systems, ground control stations, and radar sites near the Strait of Hormuz; the IRGC said attacks hit a telecommunications tower, water tanks, and the cities of Jask, Sirik, and Qeshm island.
- Jordan's military said it shot down five missiles fired from Iran; an air raid alert was issued in Bahrain.
- US President Donald Trump had accused Iran of shooting down the helicopter and vowed a mandatory response; the two Apache crew members survived and were rescued by an American sea drone.
- Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi warned: "Leave our region if you want to be safe." Iran's top negotiator in US peace talks had earlier signalled retaliation, posting: "We prefer the language of diplomacy, but we speak other languages far more fluently."
- The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed since the US and Israel launched first strikes on Iran in late February 2026.
- The exchange is complicating Trump's efforts to reach a deal to end the US-Israel-Iran war.

**Why it matters:** This is the most direct US-Iranian military exchange since the broader conflict resumed. The simultaneous Iranian retaliation against multiple US bases — including in Jordan — signals Tehran is willing to absorb American counterstrikes and respond in kind. The Hormuz closure continues to affect global energy shipping.

### Israeli air strikes hit Lebanese city of Tyre despite Iranian warning to stop attacks

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-09
**Category:** Middle East

**Summary:**
Israel carried out air strikes across southern Lebanon, including on the city of Tyre, killing at least eight people, despite a warning from Iran not to continue attacks. The strikes came after Israel and Iran had agreed to pause hostilities on Monday following their first exchange of fire since the April truce. The Israeli military also issued its first-ever evacuation order for Tyre's Christian quarter.

**Key points:**
- Lebanon's health ministry put the death toll from Tuesday's strikes at eight in Tyre, with 32 injured; at least five more were killed in strikes on Syrian nationals in villages north of Tyre.
- Israel and Iran had paused hostilities on Monday after an Israeli strike on Beirut targeting Hezbollah triggered their first exchange since the April ceasefire.
- Iran's warning that it could resume attacks if Israel did not stop attacks in Lebanon has so far gone unheeded; Israel vowed to continue its campaign against Hezbollah.
- The conflict is complicating President Trump's efforts to negotiate a broader deal to end the US-Israel-Iran war.
- Israeli military chief of staff Lt Gen Eyal Zamir said forces were continuing to dismantle Hezbollah infrastructure in southern Lebanon.

**Why it matters:** The ceasefire between Israel and Iran is fragile. Each Israeli strike in Lebanon risks triggering Iranian retaliation, which in turn could draw the US deeper into direct conflict with Tehran. The breakdown of the pause underscores how the various threads of the Middle East conflict remain deeply intertwined.

## China / Taiwan / South China Sea

### Japan-Philippines strengthen defence ties with naval vessel transfers and missile talks

**Source:** ISW, citing Philippine and Japanese official announcements — 2026-06-05
**Category:** South China Sea

**Summary:**
Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. met Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi in Tokyo on May 28 and announced a series of defence agreements to deepen military cooperation, including the planned transfer of Japanese naval vessels and negotiations over anti-ship missiles to the Philippines.

**Key points:**
- The Philippines seeks to acquire six "Mogami"-class destroyers from Japan, specialized for anti-submarine warfare, as part of efforts to expand and modernize its surface fleet.
- Japan and the Philippines agreed to accelerate talks on military intelligence sharing and to upgrade their relationship to a "Comprehensive Strategic Partnership."
- Japan will begin discussions with the Philippines over the transfer of Type-88 anti-ship missiles.
- The two nations also agreed to work toward delimiting their exclusive economic zones (EEZ) in the Western Pacific, east of Taiwan — an area that overlaps with PRC and Taiwanese EEZ claims.
- ISW assesses that enhanced cooperation will improve both nations' ability to counter Chinese coercion in the South China Sea and first island chain.

**Why it matters:** Japan is emerging as a major security partner for the Philippines, filling a gap left by reduced US direct presence. The naval cooperation and EEZ delimitation talks directly address Chinese maritime expansion in the first island chain. The intelligence-sharing agreement will give both countries better awareness of PLA deployments in the region.

### Taiwan developing AI-based drone navigation to counter GPS jamming

**Source:** ISW China & Taiwan Update — 2026-06-05
**Category:** Taiwan

**Summary:**
Taiwan's Aerospace Industrial Development Corporation (AIDC) is leading an initiative to develop AI-powered drone navigation systems resilient to GPS jamming and electronic warfare — capabilities the PLA is expected to employ in any invasion scenario.

**Key points:**
- AIDC chairs the Taiwan Excellence Drone International Business Opportunities Alliance (TEDIBOA), an industry consortium of over 200 Taiwanese drone manufacturers established by Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs in 2024.
- AIDC developed the AIxVNAV navigation system using tools licensed from US firm Vantor, using visual navigation based on satellite 3D mapping data and AI to allow drones to operate in GPS-denied environments.
- Russia and Ukraine have both used GPS jamming and spoofing to disrupt enemy drones; the PLA possesses advanced electronic warfare capabilities and views spectrum dominance as a precondition for amphibious invasion.
- ISW assesses that Taiwan's successful deployment of EW-resistant drone systems at scale will be critical to effectively locating, surveilling, and striking PLA forces in a conflict.

**Why it matters:** An amphibious invasion of Taiwan would require the PLA to suppress Taiwan's air defence and drone capabilities through intensive electronic warfare. Taiwan's push to build indigenous EW-resistant drones represents a significant hedge against this scenario and a reduction of dependence on vulnerable GPS-dependent systems.

### Dutch warship transits Taiwan Strait; PLA condemns passage as illegal

**Source:** Google News (SCMP, NL Times) — 2026-06-06
**Category:** Taiwan Strait

**Summary:**
The Netherlands became the latest nation to have a warship transit the Taiwan Strait, drawing sharp condemnation from Beijing, which claimed the passage was "illegal." The PLA said it tracked the Dutch vessel throughout its transit.

**Key points:**
- A Dutch warship crossed the Taiwan Strait in late May, triggering protests from Beijing. The PLA's Eastern Theater Command said it tracked the vessel and condemned the transit as illegal.
- The Netherlands joined other nations including the US, Canada, Australia, and the UK in conducting freedom-of-navigation operations through the Strait.
- Separately, a Chinese flotilla surged east of Taiwan amid an ongoing spat with the Philippines and Japan, according to USNI News.
- Chinese military jets also circled Taiwan during the Computex tech summit, the world's largest AI supply chain gathering, in an apparent show of force.

**Why it matters:** The pattern of increasing foreign warship transits through the Taiwan Strait — and China's increasingly strident responses — reflects growing international pushback against Chinese maritime claims. The Computex timing of PLA air operations underscores how Beijing links technology competition with military deterrence around Taiwan.
