### 1. OpenAI Unveils Lockdown Mode for Enterprise Prompt Injection Protection

**Source:** TechCrunch —2026-06-06
**Category:** AI Security / Enterprise AI

**Summary:**
OpenAI announced Lockdown Mode, a new feature designed to protect sensitive data from prompt injection attacks — where malicious instructions are hidden in webpages, uploaded files, or other content sources that AI models process. The feature restricts several live data access capabilities to reduce the attack surface for enterprise users handling sensitive information.

**Key points:**
- Disables live web browsing (cached content only), image retrieval from the web, deep research, and agent mode
- Targets businesses and organizations handling sensitive data concerned about data exfiltration via prompt injection
- OpenAI acknowledges Lockdown Mode is "not intended for everyone" — designed specifically for data exfiltration risk scenarios
- Rolling out to self-serve ChatGPT Business accounts and eligible personal accounts
- OpenAI notes it does not eliminate all prompt injection risk — cached web content or uploaded files could still affect model behavior
- First explicit product-level response from a major frontier lab to the growing threat of adversarial prompt injection in enterprise deployments

**Why it matters:**
Prompt injection has emerged as a critical enterprise trust barrier for AI deployments — attackers can embed hidden instructions in web content, documents, or emails that AI assistants inadvertently follow. Lockdown Mode is the first major security feature from a frontier lab that explicitly acknowledges and mitigates this attack vector at the product level. As AI agents are increasingly deployed to handle email, documents, and web research autonomously, this signals a shift from theoretical prompt injection concerns to concrete product defense.

### 2. WWDC 2026 Preview: Siri's Overhaul, AI Agent App Store, and Apple Intelligence Expands

**Source:** TechCrunch —2026-06-06
**Category:** Consumer AI / Big Tech Product Launches

**Summary:**
Apple's Worldwide Developers Conference kicks off June 7, 2026 with the most significant Siri overhaul in its history — rebuilt on Google Gemini technology to create a more conversational, context-aware assistant capable of multi-step task handling. Apple is also introducing an AI agent integration layer for the App Store, a redesigned Visual Intelligence camera mode, and broader Apple Intelligence features across Photos, Image Playground, and Wallet.

**Key points:**
- Siri being rebuilt with Google Gemini under the hood — marking a major strategic AI partnership
- Standalone Siri app expected to compete directly with ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini
- AI agent App Store integration: users can delegate tasks (booking, document editing, smart home control) to AI agents that interact with apps on their behalf
- Visual Intelligence section in Camera app taking over from the previous Camera Control button feature
- Photos app getting AI scene optimization, automatic object removal, and natural-language AI editing
- Image Playground upgraded: higher quality generation, better character consistency, "describe a change" editing
- Wallet app adding bill-splitting via receipt photo, and "Create a Pass" from physical items
- Privacy-conscious design expected to be a key differentiator vs. other AI assistants

**Why it matters:**
Apple's AI strategy has been perceived as lagging behind OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic. WWDC 2026 marks a decisive pivot — leveraging Google's Gemini technology to close the capability gap while maintaining Apple's privacy brand. The AI agent App Store integration is particularly significant: if Apple can establish a trusted agent marketplace with clear permissions and user control, it could define the consumer AI agent standard the same way the original App Store defined mobile apps. The Gemini partnership also signals a rare strategic reliance on a direct competitor for core technology.

### 3. Trump Administration Explores Equity Stake in OpenAI; Public Wealth Fund Proposal

**Source:** TechCrunch — 2026-06-06
**Category:** AI Policy / Government& Big Tech

**Summary:**
The Trump administration has been discussing taking an equity stake in OpenAI as part of a broader initiative that could see citizens receive a direct share in AI-driven growth. The concept, which OpenAI itself proposed as a "Public Wealth Fund," would distribute proceeds from AI companies to the public — drawing both bipartisan interest and criticism from those concerned about corporate-government fusion.

**Key points:**
- Trump confirmed he has spoken to AI companies about deals "where the American people can benefit from the success of AI"
- CNBC reported the administration is discussing an equity stake in OpenAI specifically
- OpenAI proposed seeding a Public Wealth Fund with equity, distributing AI-driven gains directly to citizens
- Senator Bernie Sanders separately proposed a one-time 50% tax on AI companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI) payable in stock — also aimed at public ownership
- This follows the government taking a 10% stake in Intel in 2025 as a pattern of direct equity ownership in tech companies
- SpaceX (now incorporating xAI) is also in the mix — its IPO at ~$1.75T valuation is expected around the same time
- David Sacks (former AI/crypto czar, now PCAST co-chair) acknowledged resonance of the idea but warned it accelerates "corporate-government fusion"
- Dare Obasanjo suggested "the groundwork is already being laid for a government bailout of OpenAI"

**Why it matters:**
This represents a fundamental shift in how the US government approaches AI companies — from regulator to partial owner. If the administration takes equity in OpenAI (and potentially other AI labs), it creates a direct financial interest in AI companies' valuations and success, raising questions about regulatory independence, competitive dynamics, and whether government ownership shapes AI development priorities. The Public Wealth Fund concept also introduces a novel redistribution mechanism for AI gains — potentially precedent-setting globally. The Intel precedent suggests this is not merely theoretical.
