## Ukraine / Russia

### Russia Makes Tactical Gains in Kostyantynivka, Ukraine Strikes Deep Inside Russia

**Source:** BBC / Institute for the Study of War — 2026-06-10/11
**Category:** Ukraine / Russia

**Summary:**
Russian forces are advancing on Kostyantynivka, which remains Moscow's assessed main effort for the Spring-Summer 2026 offensive. According to ISW, two Russian tactical groups — the "Bakhmut" group (3rd Army Corps) and the "Dzerzhinsk" group (8th Combined Arms Army and supporting elements) — have advanced from the north and south into eastern parts of the city, coming within roughly 2km of each other. ISW estimates Russian forces have been infiltrating the city since October 2025 and now control approximately 12.69% of Kostyantynivka. Meanwhile, Ukraine struck a Russian military plant deep inside Russia, a Russian oil refinery, and a "shadow fleet" oil tanker in the Black Sea.

**Key points:**
- Russian "Bakhmut" tactical group advanced from Stupochky through Novodmytrivka into northeastern Kostyantynivka along the T-0504 Bakhmut road; "Dzerzhinsk" group advanced from Illinivka into northwestern and southwestern parts of the city
- ISW assesses the "Dzerzhinsk" group has likely achieved a tactical breakthrough in the central-western part of the city
- Russian forces have failed to seize the Kostyantynivka railway station itself; Ukrainian forces cleared Dovha Blaka of Russian infiltrators
- Russia launched its campaign for Kostyantynivka in Summer 2025 after seizing Toretsk and Chasiv Yar; the 14-month Toretsk campaign ended August 2025
- Ukraine's military says it struck a military plant deep inside Russia, a Russian oil refinery, and a Black Sea "shadow fleet" tanker
- Russia replenished ~80% of units attacking Kostyantynivka as of June 6, 2026

**Why it matters:** Kostyantynivka is a key remaining Ukrainian stronghold before the wider Donbas highway network. Its fall would expose Ukrainian positions further west and represent a significant symbolic and strategic defeat ahead of the war's third full year.

## Middle East

### US and Iran Exchange Strikes for Second Consecutive Day, Ceasefire "Practically Meaningless"

**Source:** BBC — 2026-06-11
**Category:** Middle East / Iran

**Summary:**
The US and Iran exchanged strikes across the Middle East for a second consecutive day on June 11, further straining the shaky ceasefire agreed in April. US Central Command (Centcom) completed a wave of "self-defense strikes" targeting military, surveillance and radar sites in southern Iran, hours after President Trump vowed US forces would hit Iran "hard." Iran responded by targeting US military assets in Bahrain, Kuwait, and Jordan with missiles and drones. Iran's foreign ministry said the overnight attacks violated the two-month-old ceasefire, rendering it "practically meaningless."

**Key points:**
- Centcom struck military, surveillance and radar sites in southern Iran; hours earlier Trump warned "We hit them hard yesterday and we're going to hit them hard again today"
- Iran's IRGC fired 12 ballistic missiles at the Muwaffaq Salti Airbase in Jordan, claiming to have destroyed US fighter jets and facilities; Jordanian state media said 20 missiles were intercepted with no casualties
- Bahrain's interior ministry said falling shrapnel from intercepted Iranian drones damaged homes and vehicles in Manama and Hamad Town; an 11-year-old girl was treated for minor injury
- Kuwait said it intercepted "hostile aerial targets" and temporarily closed its airspace
- Explosions reported around Tehran, Bandar Abbas, and areas near the Strait of Hormuz; IRGC said it hit two oil tankers passing through the Strait, though Centcom said commercial shipping continues
- Iran's foreign ministry said the attacks "violated the ceasefire" and responsibility for "extremely serious consequences" lay with US leaders
- Brent crude oil climbed to ~$95/barrel following the Strait of Hormuz incident

**Why it matters:** The April ceasefire is unraveling rapidly. Both sides are calibrating responses to avoid full re-escalation while demonstrating resolve — but the exchange of strikes across multiple US-aligned states signals the conflict is spreading beyond a bilateral US-Iran框架.

### Three Indian Sailors Killed in US Strike on Oil Tanker Near Oman

**Source:** BBC — 2026-06-11
**Category:** Middle East / Iran

**Summary:**
Three Indian sailors have been confirmed dead after the US military struck the Palau-flagged MT Settebello tanker in the Gulf of Oman. The vessel came under attack on Wednesday after the US accused it of not complying with directions and "attempting to transport oil from Iran." There were 24 Indian crew members aboard; 21 were rescued by Omani forces. India summoned the deputy head of the US mission in Delhi and called for an end to the targeting of commercial shipping. This is the second vessel with an Indian crew hit by US forces this week — the MT Marivex was struck on Monday with all 24 crew rescued by Oman.

**Key points:**
- US Central Command said an aircraft fired "precision munitions" into the engine room of the Settebello "after the crew repeatedly failed" to follow directions
- India called the incident "deeply unfortunate"; the shipping minister said bodies would be repatriated soon
- The US has blocked access to Iran's ports since effectively closing the Strait of Hormuz during the conflict; Centcom says 8 vessels have been disabled and 134 redirected since April 13
- India's government: "targeting of commercial shipping and civilian infrastructure in the region must end"
- Oil prices rose ~2% following the incident and the Strait of Hormuz closure announcement

**Why it matters:** Civilian mariners are increasingly caught in the crossfire of the US-Iran maritime blockade. India's condemnation of the strike adds diplomatic pressure on the US as the conflict expands into commercial shipping lanes.

### Israeli Strikes in Southern Lebanon Kill 17; Hezbollah Launches Retaliatory Attacks

**Source:** BBC — 2026-06-11
**Category:** Middle East / Lebanon

**Summary:**
Israeli air and artillery strikes killed at least 17 people across southern Lebanon on June 10, Lebanese state media reported. Nine were killed in strikes in the town of Tayr Debba just east of Tyre; three others died in Deir Qanoun el-Nahr and Seddiqin. An Israeli drone strike on a car in Sidon killed two more. Hezbollah said it carried out rocket barrages against Israeli troop positions in the southern Bayada and Yohmor areas. UN human rights chief Volker Türk is sending a team of investigators to Lebanon at the Lebanese government's request to examine possible war crimes.

**Key points:**
- Lebanon's health ministry says at least 3,696 people have been killed in the country since the conflict began; 30 Israeli soldiers and 4 civilians killed on the Israeli side
- Almost 1 million Lebanese remain displaced — a fifth of the population; 1.4 million need humanitarian aid according to the UN
- The US-brokered ceasefire between Israeli and Lebanese governments broke down within weeks of being signed on April 16
- Iran is demanding any US-Iran deal also cover Lebanon, complicating broader negotiations with Trump
- Israel issued a new evacuation order for Tyre including its Christian quarter for the first time on June 9

**Why it matters:** Lebanon remains a secondary front in the US-Iran war. The killing of 17 people in a single day underscores that the Lebanon conflict is intensifying rather than cooling, and Iran's demand to link Lebanon to any broader ceasefire talks threatens to expand the scope of negotiations.

### Analysis: Trump and Netanyahu's War on Iran Risks Permanent Crisis

**Source:** BBC — 2026-06-09
**Category:** Middle East / Analysis

**Summary:**
International Editor Jeremy Bowen writes that the US-Israel war on Iran, launched on February 28, 2026, has failed to achieve its stated objectives. The Iranian regime has survived and demonstrated continued ability to strike US assets across the region. Both Trump and Netanyahu issued video statements on February 28 portraying victory as imminent; neither has delivered it. The risk now is of a "long, attritional permacrisis" that lurches in and out of outright conflict, with no clear exit strategy.

**Key points:**
- Iran's downing of a US Army Apache helicopter (with surviving crew) prompted the latest round of strikes, showing Tehran can still inflict pain on American forces
- Trump sought a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and address Iran's enriched uranium stockpile; the war is unpopular in America ahead of midterms
- Netanyahu's strategy of using overwhelming force against Iran's regional network has failed to break Hezbollah or Iranian resolve
- Israel called off a planned strike on Beirut after Trump intervened; Israeli press described Netanyahu as looking like a "deflated balloon"
- The Strait of Hormuz — once one of the world's busiest waterways — has effectively been closed since February

**Why it matters:** This piece captures the strategic failure of the original US-Israel logic for the war. The conflict is now in a grinding, multi-theater phase with no diplomatic off-ramp visible.

## South China Sea / Taiwan Strait

### Japan-Philippines Announce Major Defense Agreements; EEZ Delimitation Talks Planned

**Source:** Institute for the Study of War / Google News — 2026-06-05 and ongoing
**Category:** South China Sea / Taiwan

**Summary:**
Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. met Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi in Tokyo on May 28 and announced a slate of defense agreements to strengthen the US-aligned front against Chinese coercion in the Western Pacific. The two sides agreed to accelerate talks on military intelligence sharing, the transfer of Japanese naval vessels (including six anti-submarine warfare destroyers), and the potential transfer of Type 88 anti-ship missiles. Japan and the Philippines also announced plans to negotiate delimitation of their exclusive economic zones in the Western Pacific east of Taiwan — overlapping with PRC and Taiwan's claimed EEZs. The two nations upgraded their relationship to a "Comprehensive Strategic Partnership."

**Key points:**
- The Philippines seeks six Japanese "Mogami"-class destroyers specialized for anti-submarine warfare
- Military intelligence sharing agreement would give both nations better awareness of PLA deployments in the first island chain
- Japan and the Philippines both adhere to the one-China policy, making official engagement with Taiwan unlikely
- EEZ delimitation negotiations highlight growing cooperation; the overlap east of Taiwan sits in a strategically critical area
- ISW assesses enhanced Japan-Philippines cooperation will constrain PLA freedom of maneuver and require additional Chinese resources to offset

**Why it matters:** Japan's deepening military partnership with the Philippines is one of the most significant developments in the first island chain's balance of power in 2026. The planned EEZ delimitation, even without Taiwan's participation, formalizes a rules-based maritime order that excludes China's expansive nine-dash line claims.
