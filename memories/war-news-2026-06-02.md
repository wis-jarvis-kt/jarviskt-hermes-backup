## Ukraine / Russia

### Russia launches mass drone and missile attack on Ukraine, killing at least 18

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-02
**Category:** Ukraine/Russia

**Summary:**
Russia carried out one of its largest assaults in recent months, launching 656 strike drones and 73 missiles of various types — ballistic, cruise, and anti-ship — across Ukraine overnight. At least 18 people were killed, including two children. Kyiv was the worst hit, with six dead, while 12 people died in Dnipro after an apartment block was struck. More than 100 people were injured.

**Key points:**
- Targets included civilian infrastructure and energy facilities across multiple cities
- An eight-year-old boy and a woman were pulled from the rubble of a collapsed apartment building in Dnipro
- Russia's defence ministry said the strikes were a response to Ukrainian attacks, claiming all objectives were met
- Moscow had vowed "systematic strikes" after accusing Kyiv of a deadly attack on a student dormitory in occupied eastern Ukraine
- President Zelensky urged the US to supply Patriot missiles, which are in short supply due to demand from the US-Israel war against Iran
- Blackouts were reported across Kyiv; fires broke out near a petrol station, construction site, and residential buildings

**Why it matters:** The attack represents a significant escalation in Russia's targeting of civilian infrastructure and comes amid heightened tensions, with Moscow warning of systematic retaliation. Shortage of air defence interceptors remains a critical vulnerability for Ukraine.

## Middle East

### Clashes continue in Lebanon despite Israel and Hezbollah accepting US partial ceasefire plan

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-02
**Category:** Middle East

**Summary:**
Fighting has continued in southern Lebanon despite both Israel and Hezbollah accepting a US-proposed partial ceasefire. President Trump announced both sides had agreed to stop shooting, but Israel said it would continue strikes on Beirut's suburbs if Hezbollah attacked Israeli civilians, and that its forces would continue operating in south Lebanon. Overnight, Hezbollah targeted Israeli tanks with missiles and shells, and the Israeli military intercepted two projectiles fired from Lebanon.

**Key points:**
- Iran's foreign minister warned that Israeli actions in Lebanon violated the US-Iran ceasefire and said Tehran could suspend indirect negotiations with Washington
- Iran warned it and its allies could "activate other fronts, including the Bab al-Mandab Strait" at the entrance to the Red Sea
- Trump insisted talks with Iran were continuing "at a rapid pace" and said no troops were heading to Beirut
- Lebanon was drawn into the US-Israel-Iran war on 2 March when Hezbollah fired rockets in retaliation for an Israeli strike that killed Iran's supreme leader
- At least 3,433 people have been killed in Lebanon since the start of the war

**Why it matters:** The partial ceasefire is fragile. Iran's warning that the Lebanon conflict could collapse the broader US-Iran truce underscores the interconnectedness of multiple fronts. The Bab al-Mandab threat signals that Iran and its allies may seek to expand pressure beyond Lebanon.

### Israel added to UN blacklist for sexual violence in warzones for first time

**Source:** BBC News — 2026-06-02
**Category:** Middle East

**Summary:**
The UN has placed Israel on its annual blacklist of countries that commit sexual violence in warzones for the first time. The UN verified 31 cases of sexual violence by Israeli forces against Palestinians from Gaza and the West Bank — 13 in 2025 and 18 in prior years. The violations included rape, gang rape, forced nudity, and violence to genitals, committed by members of the Israeli military, police, and prison service.

**Key points:**
- Israel rejected the allegations and said it would break off relations with the UN secretary general's office
- Israel's ambassador accused Secretary General Guterres of spreading antisemitic lies
- The UN said its attempts to investigate were obstructed by the Israeli government and threats made against detainees
- In one widely reported case, five Israeli guards were caught on CCTV apparently sexually abusing a Palestinian detainee at Sde Teiman; Israel's top military lawyer dropped charges against them in March 2026
- Hamas was also added to the same UN report for sexual violence against hostages taken on 7 October 2023

**Why it matters:** The designation is a significant international reputational marker and could affect Israel's standing with multilateral institutions. The timing — amid ongoing Gaza operations and the Lebanon escalation — compounds the diplomatic pressure on Israel.

## South China Sea / Taiwan Strait

### China patrols near disputed South China Sea waters; allies boost Taiwan Strait transits while US takes different route

**Source:** Taipei Times / South China Morning Post — 2026-06-01
**Category:** South China Sea / Taiwan Strait

**Summary:**
China's military has been conducting patrols near disputed waters in the South China Sea, according to recent reports. Meanwhile, several US allies — including Canada — have been transit operations through the Taiwan Strait. A notable development is that the US itself appears to be taking a different routing approach compared to its allies, a dynamic examined in detail by the South China Morning Post.

**Key points:**
- A Canadian warship recently transited the Taiwan Strait ahead of a Chinese minister's visit, signalling allied solidarity with Taiwan
- Allied nations have been increasing their naval presence in the Taiwan Strait as a show of support
- China's navy is reportedly shifting pressure tactics beyond the Taiwan Strait itself, expanding its operational scope
- Analysts note a divergence between US and allied approach to the Strait, with Washington potentially adopting a lower-profile posture
- The Global Taiwan Institute published analysis warning that the South China Sea could become China's next target following Taiwan

**Why it matters:** The combination of increased Chinese assertiveness in the South China Sea and shifting dynamics in Taiwan Strait transits reflects growing tension in the First Island Chain. The divergence between US and allied postures is being closely watched by regional partners including the Philippines, Japan, and Taiwan.
