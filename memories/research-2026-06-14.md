## 3. KPMG Pulls Report on AI Usage Due to Apparent Hallucinations

- **Source:** TechCrunch (June 14, 2026) — IN BRIEF, by Anthony Ha — 17 hours ago
- **Category:** AI Reliability / Enterprise AI
- **Summary:** KPMG pulled a published report on AI usage after discovering it contained apparent AI hallucinations — fabricated data, citations, or claims that the AI system invented. The firm had apparently relied on an AI system to help compile or write the report, which then generated plausible-sounding but false information. The incident adds to the growing evidence that LLMs can confidently produce false content that appears credible.
- **Significance:** A Big Four accounting firm — not a random user, not a small startup, but KPMG — being burned by AI hallucinations in a published report is a significant data point for enterprise AI adoption. This is exactly the failure mode that makes AI hallucination a serious concern for knowledge work: the output looks professional, the citations look real, and the errors aren't obvious until someone with domain expertise digs in. It raises the question of whether any organization can safely use AI-generated content without extensive human fact-checking, which partially defeats the efficiency purpose. For AI vendors selling into enterprise, this is a reminder that "AI-assisted" workflows need robust guardrails.
